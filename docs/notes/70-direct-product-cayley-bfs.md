# Direct-product Cayley graphs and BFS shuffle signatures

This note connects the Cartesian-product geometry of note 69 to Cayley BFS.
It is an algebraic interpretation of exact traversal records, not a proposal
for recognizing products or optimizing a search implementation.

## Exact convention

Let `G` and `H` be groups with identities `e_G,e_H`. Fix right multiplication
and directed generator alphabets `S` and `T`. Give the two alphabets disjoint
labels even if their printed names happen to coincide. On `G x H`, use

```text
U = {(s,e_H): s in S} union {(e_G,t): t in T}.
```

Then the labeled directed Cayley graph `Cay(G x H,U)` is the Cartesian product
of `Cay(G,S)` and `Cay(H,T)`: an `S` edge changes only the first coordinate and
a `T` edge changes only the second.

This remains true for inverse-closed alphabets and their undirected reading.
For a left Cayley convention the same statement holds if every factor and the
product consistently use left multiplication. Mixing left and right action is
not a harmless notation change for replay labels.

Identity generators create loops. Duplicate generator labels can create
parallel labeled occurrences. The product statement still works for the
corresponding labeled multidigraph, but collapsing it to a simple graph changes
occurrence and word counts even though state distances may stay unchanged.

## Directed reachability and additive distance

With non-symmetric alphabets, BFS explores the positive generator monoid from
the source; it need not reach the whole group. A product element `(g,h)` is
reachable from `(e_G,e_H)` exactly when `g` and `h` are reachable in their
factor digraphs. When both distances are finite,

```text
dist_U((e_G,e_H),(g,h)) = dist_S(e_G,g) + dist_T(e_H,h).
```

This is note 69's projection proof applied to Cayley coordinates. If either
factor distance is infinite, the product distance is infinite. Therefore the
rooted spherical growth series of the reachable product component is

```text
Gamma_U(z) = Gamma_S(z) Gamma_T(z).
```

This is an exact formal-series identity, not an extrapolation from a finite
frontier prefix.

## Why cross-factor letters commute

For every `s in S` and `t in T`,

```text
(s,e_H)(e_G,t) = (s,t) = (e_G,t)(s,e_H).
```

Thus any adjacent cross-factor letters may swap without changing the endpoint.
If a shortest factor word for `g` has length `i` and one for `h` has length
`j`, every shuffle preserving the internal order of each word is a shortest
product word. For tagged labeled words,

```text
sigma_U(g,h) = choose(i+j,i) sigma_S(g) sigma_T(h).
```

The formula assumes `sigma` counts all factor shortest labeled words and that
parallel labels remain distinct. For vertex-sequence paths in a simple graph,
use the corresponding factor vertex-path counts instead.

## The first duplicate signature

Take one nonidentity distance-one generator from each factor. At product depth
two, the records

```text
(e,e) --s--> (s,e) --t--> (s,t)
(e,e) --t--> (e,t) --s--> (s,t)
```

arrive from distinct depth-one parents at the same state. This is candidate
convergence caused by an interleaving equality. It is not merely two historical
words merging before the current frontier: both predecessor states are still
distinct.

At greater depths, three multiplicities must still be separated:

1. shortest labeled words, including all shuffles;
2. candidate records from already deduplicated parent states;
3. unique endpoint states admitted to the next frontier.

Static trace classes capture every cross-factor shuffle because the
cross-factor independence relation is complete bipartite. Internal factor
relations can cause further equality inside a trace class or between classes.

## Converse failures

### Commutation does not prove a direct product

The cyclic group `Z_4` is abelian. Its subgroups generated by `1` and `2`
commute, but they overlap: `<2>` is contained in `<1>`. The multiplication map
from the proposed two factors is not injective. Cross-commutation alone lacks
the trivial-intersection and generation conditions needed for an internal
direct product.

Consequently, observing `st=ts` duplicate signatures does not prove that the
whole Cayley graph factorizes.

### A different generator set can couple coordinates

Even on the group `G x H`, adding a generator `(s,t)` that changes both
coordinates adds a diagonal edge. The resulting Cayley graph is no longer the
Cartesian product under that generator metric. Its group is unchanged, but its
BFS distances, layers, and path counts change.

### A quotient action can erase the product

A Schreier action may identify several group elements as one visible state. If
the stabilizer is not itself compatible with a product decomposition, visible
state equality can couple the coordinates. The group-level shuffle equality
remains sound, but the visible graph can contain additional merges and need not
have product sphere counts.

## What can and cannot be inferred for CayleyPy

Safe interpretation:

- independently acting move families predict sound word equalities;
- their interleavings can explain a portion of same-state candidate
  convergence;
- a declared direct-product state model predicts an exact convolution of
  semantic BFS sphere counts.

Unsafe interpretation:

- pairwise commuting moves prove a global product decomposition;
- a product decomposition of the abstract group proves that the chosen
  generator metric is Cartesian;
- algebraically adjacent equal keys will be adjacent in a warp, batch, rank, or
  network message;
- a semantic convolution predicts runtime, memory traffic, or multi-GPU
  balance;
- agreement of a few measured frontier sizes proves product structure.

Physical duplicate locality depends on frontier order, expansion layout,
partitioning, and batching. Product algebra determines which records are equal,
not where an implementation places them.
