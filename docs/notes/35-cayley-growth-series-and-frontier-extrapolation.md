# Cayley growth series: encoding frontiers is not predicting them

For a fixed Cayley graph, BFS sphere sizes are coefficients of a formal power
series.  In structured families this yields exact formulas and recurrences.
It does not make an empirically observed prefix safe to extrapolate: arbitrarily
long identical prefixes can have completely different tails.

This note studies the algebraic view of BFS geometry.  It does not replace
traversal by a forecasting algorithm.

## Spherical and ball series

Let `G` be generated by a finite symmetric set `S`, with the declared simple or
labeled Cayley convention fixed.  Write

```text
w_d = |{g in G | length_S(g)=d}|
V_d = sum_(0<=i<=d) w_i.
```

The spherical and ball growth series are

```text
Sigma_S(z) = sum_(d>=0) w_d z^d
Beta_S(z)  = sum_(d>=0) V_d z^d.
```

Because `V_d` is the prefix sum of `w_d`, as formal series

```text
Beta_S(z) = Sigma_S(z)/(1-z).
```

These coefficients are exactly exhaustive identity-rooted BFS frontier and
visited sizes.  They are not the `|S|^d` generator-word counts from note 33.

For a finite connected Cayley graph of diameter `D`, `Sigma_S` is a polynomial
of degree `D`; `V_d=|G|` for all `d>=D`, so the indefinitely extended ball
series still has the `(1-z)` denominator.

## Rational growth and recurrences

The spherical series is rational when

```text
Sigma_S(z) = P(z)/Q(z)
```

for polynomials with `Q(0) != 0`.  After normalizing `Q(0)=1`, coefficient
comparison shows that `w_d` satisfies a fixed finite linear recurrence once
past the numerator's degree.  Conversely, an eventually constant-coefficient
linear recurrence yields a rational generating series.

This is an algebraic theorem about the complete coefficient sequence.  Fitting
a recurrence to a finite trace does not prove that the recurrence continues.
Many rational functions, polynomials, and non-rational series share any chosen
finite prefix.

Rational growth is also not universal merely because the graph is Cayley.
It is known for important classes and generating sets, including Coxeter and
automatic-group settings, but it remains a property requiring a theorem under
the actual generator convention.

## Exact examples

### Free group

For the free group of rank `r` with its `2r` standard generators and inverses,
the first letter has `2r` choices and every later reduced letter has `2r-1`:

```text
w_0 = 1
w_d = 2r(2r-1)^(d-1), d>=1

Sigma(z) = 1 + 2r z/(1-(2r-1)z).
```

This is the non-backtracking tree sphere count.  Arithmetic exact-length walk
mass remains `(2r)^d` because it includes inverse returns.

### Infinite integer lattice

For `Z` with generators `{+1,-1}`,

```text
Sigma_Z(z) = 1 + 2z + 2z^2 + ... = (1+z)/(1-z).
```

For `Z^n` with signed coordinate generators, word length is the `L1` norm.
Each coordinate contributes `sum_(k in Z) z^|k|=(1+z)/(1-z)`, hence

```text
Sigma_(Z^n)(z) = ((1+z)/(1-z))^n.
```

The coefficients grow polynomially despite `2n` generator choices at every
state; commutation and cancellation merge the word tree heavily.

### Hypercube

For `(Z_2)^n` with coordinate-flip generators, distance is Hamming weight:

```text
Sigma_Qn(z) = (1+z)^n,
w_d = binomial(n,d).
```

The polynomial terminates at diameter `n`.

### Adjacent-transposition symmetric group

For `S_n` generated by adjacent swaps, distance is inversion count and

```text
Sigma_Sn(z)
  = product_(i=1)^n (1+z+...+z^(i-1)).
```

This finite polynomial is palindromic of degree `n(n-1)/2`.  The exact `S_8`
frontiers recorded in note 10 are its coefficients.  Their symmetry is a
proved involution on permutations, not generic evidence that finite BFS
profiles are bell-shaped.

## Generator dependence

The exact series belongs to `(G,S)`, not to the abstract group alone.  Adding a
generator can shorten many elements and move coefficients between spheres.
Adding an identity or duplicate labeled generator may leave simple-state
distances unchanged while changing labeled transition or word counts.

For infinite finitely generated groups, broad growth type is stable under
changing finite generating sets up to coarse comparison, but exact coefficients,
recurrences, peaks, and hardware workload are not.  Capacity planning needs the
actual move set, action direction, legality, and quotient convention.

## An arbitrarily long false extrapolation

Compare

```text
Cay(Z,{+1,-1})
```

with the cycle

```text
Cay(Z_N,{+1,-1}).
```

For every depth strictly below `N/2`, both identity-rooted sphere sequences are

```text
1,2,2,2,...
```

Yet the infinite line continues forever, while the finite cycle reaches one or
two antipodal terminal vertices and then its frontier becomes empty.  Given any
finite observation radius `R`, choosing `N>2R` makes the traces identical
through `R` and different later.

Therefore no finite prefix alone certifies:

- finiteness or infiniteness;
- diameter or remaining levels;
- future peak frontier;
- eventual recurrence;
- rational versus non-rational growth;
- capacity required beyond the observed ball.

This is the Cayley version of local indistinguishability.  The missing evidence
is a global group/graph theorem, not more confidence in the fitted curve.

## Relations and onset of deviation

A quotient of a free group by a long relator can remain tree-like within a
large radius.  The relator becomes visible only when distinct short words begin
to identify or return.  Note 27's girth thresholds formalize part of this local
window.

But a shortest relation does not determine the full growth series.  Relations
overlap, conjugate, and combine; the same girth can coexist with different
later sphere sequences.  A finite automaton for a proved geodesic normal form
can yield a rational series, whereas merely listing presentation relators does
not automatically supply such an automaton.

## What a proved series can and cannot certify

If a formula is proved for the exact graph, it can provide:

- exact frontier and cumulative visited counts;
- diameter and peak frontier for a finite polynomial;
- consistency checks for measured layer artifacts;
- advance capacity requirements for frontier/visited records;
- expected total state expansions for exhaustive BFS.

It does not by itself provide:

- state identities or a visited representation;
- parent paths or generator words;
- candidate convergence and same-layer edge counts;
- owner-routing balance;
- memory locality or runtime;
- correctness under a different quotient or generator set.

Note 32 shows why even exact `w_d` does not recover intersection profiles.

## Capacity forecasting without pretending to optimize

A proven coefficient sequence can turn memory questions into arithmetic.  If a
frontier record uses `f` bytes and each persistent visited record uses `v`
bytes, the semantic lower-level payloads include

```text
frontier_payload(d) = f*w_d
visited_payload(d)  = v*V_d.
```

Candidate, hash-table, parent, allocator, routing, and scratch overheads remain
separate.  A formula for `w_d` is not a measured device capacity and must not be
used as one without representation evidence.

For multi-GPU search, global `w_d` also says nothing about owner skew.  A
uniform group action does not guarantee that a chosen hash or state encoding
distributes every individual sphere uniformly.

## Validation uses coefficients as checksums, not proofs from data

When an independent theorem gives `w_d`, a completed BFS trace should satisfy:

```text
observed_frontier[d] = w_d
sum_d w_d = |G|                 for finite G
last nonzero d = diameter.
```

Failure is strong evidence of a graph-convention mismatch, lost/false visited
decision, overflow, or incomplete termination.  Agreement of counts is still
not a full state-set certificate: one missing state and one incorrect state can
cancel numerically.  Replay, exact identity, and transition checks remain
necessary.

## Rejected shortcuts

- **A smooth frontier prefix determines the rest of the search.** Large cycles
  and the infinite line refute this at any finite observation radius.
- **Every Cayley growth series is rational.** Rationality needs a theorem for
  the relevant class and generating set.
- **Rational growth eliminates the need for BFS.** A recurrence counts spheres;
  it need not enumerate identities or paths.
- **The growth series is an invariant of the abstract group alone.** Exact
  coefficients depend on the generating set and graph convention.
- **Known frontier sizes determine GPU cost.** Candidate multiplicity,
  representation, locality, routing, and scratch remain unknown.
- **Matching all expected counts proves the visited set is exact.** Aggregate
  equality can hide compensating membership errors.

## Current conclusion

The spherical growth series is a compact algebraic encoding of exact BFS
frontier sizes for a fixed Cayley graph.  When independently proved, it is a
powerful oracle and capacity input.  A measured prefix, however long, is not
such a proof: locally identical Cayley graphs can saturate, continue
polynomially, or diverge only beyond the observed radius.
